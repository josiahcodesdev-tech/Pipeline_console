1. Executive Summary

The American Friends Service Committee (AFSC) operates in some of the world's most complex and under-resourced contexts — Somalia and South Sudan — where peacebuilding, healing, and social cohesion depend not only on strong programming, but on the sustained ability to attract and secure funding. Vantage Africa School of Leadership is pleased to present this technical proposal for a four-day virtual training in Fundraising and Proposal Writing, designed to strengthen AFSC's internal teams and local partner organizations in developing competitive, donor-aligned proposals.

What AFSC will gain

A practical four-day learning journey covering the full proposal development cycle — from donor mapping and concept note design to Theory of Change, logical frameworks, budgeting and writing compelling proposals that win funding.

How the programme is designed

Built around AFSC's thematic areas of Just Peace and Just Migration, the programme is contextualized for humanitarian and peacebuilding programming. It uses participatory methods, real donor case studies, live drafting exercises and peer review to ensure participants leave with usable skills and tools.

By the end of the four days, participants will be equipped to identify funding opportunities, structure concept notes and full proposals, apply Theory of Change thinking, align with donor priorities and develop budgets — with practical templates and tools they can use immediately.

2. Understanding of the Assignment

AFSC requires a training engagement that goes beyond theory. The need is for a practical, interactive programme that equips both internal staff and local partner organizations in Somalia and South Sudan to develop high-quality, competitive proposals aligned with donor priorities in the areas of Just Peace and Just Migration. Participants come with varying levels of experience, and the training must be adaptive and contextually grounded.

What AFSC is trying to solve

Variable fundraising capacity across internal teams and local partner organizations; limited skills in donor mapping, proposal structure, Theory of Change application, budgeting and concept note writing, which constrains AFSC's ability to scale peacebuilding and social cohesion programming.

What the training must achieve

Equip participants to identify funding opportunities, structure compelling proposals and concept notes, apply Theory of Change thinking, align with donor requirements and develop credible budgets — producing usable tools and drafts during the training itself.

Who should benefit

AFSC Somalia and South Sudan programme staff, local partner organizations including community- and faith-based organizations (C/FBOs), and technical officers responsible for resource mobilization and programme development.

What success looks like

Participants can independently draft a concept note or full proposal section, map relevant donors, articulate a Theory of Change, build a logical framework and apply what they have learned to live funding opportunities within AFSC's thematic areas.

The programme must therefore balance conceptual rigor with immediate practical application. Three realities shape the design:

•AFSC's work in Somalia and South Sudan sits at the intersection of peacebuilding, trauma healing, social cohesion and youth empowerment — funding proposals must reflect this complexity in ways donors find compelling.

•Partner organizations range from grassroots C/FBOs to more institutionally developed NGOs, requiring the training to be adaptable across levels of experience without losing depth.

•Participants need ready-to-use tools: templates, frameworks and reference materials they can apply immediately after the training — not generic content disconnected from their programming reality.


3. Why Fundraising Capacity Matters for AFSC's Mission

Peacebuilding, healing and social cohesion work depends on sustainable funding. In conflict-affected settings like South Sudan and Somalia, the ability to write strong, competitive proposals directly determines whether communities receive the support they need — and whether local partners can grow into credible, independent actors in the humanitarian and development landscape.

Sustained programming

Strong proposal skills enable AFSC and partners to secure multi-year, flexible funding that sustains long-term peacebuilding efforts rather than short project cycles.

Partner independence

When local C/FBOs and NGO partners can write competitive proposals, they reduce dependence on sub-granting and build their own donor relationships and institutional credibility.

Donor alignment

Understanding donor priorities — from the EU and UN to Foundations and bilateral funders — allows teams to position AFSC's work more strategically and increase success rates.

Programme quality

Theory of Change thinking and logical frameworks improve not just proposals but actual programme design, making interventions more coherent, measurable and results-oriented.

Competitive advantage

Well-written proposals with strong evidence, clear results logic and credible budgets stand out in crowded funding environments where dozens of organizations compete for the same grants.

Institutional resilience

Organizations with strong internal fundraising capacity are better positioned to absorb funding shocks, diversify their donor base and sustain operations through volatile environments.

A stronger fundraising capacity does not just bring in more money — it transforms local organizations from service recipients into strategic actors who shape their own programming, attract their own donors and lead their own development agendas.

6. Training Approach and Methodology

The programme methodology is designed as a chain of learning events: concept input, guided practice, live drafting, peer review and institutional action planning. The intention is that participants leave with usable drafts and tools, not notes alone. The fully virtual format is designed to be as interactive and participatory as an in-person workshop.

2. Concept Input

3. Live Drafting

4. Peer Review

5. Action Planning

Short facilitated input using humanitarian and peacebuilding funding examples

Structured peer feedback using a review rubric aligned to donor criteria

Consolidate learning into a personal fundraising action plan for next 90 days

Virtual Facilitation Methods

Short input sessions

Focused 20–30 minute concept presentations using humanitarian donor case studies and real proposal examples.

Breakout group work

Small groups of 4–6 work on drafting exercises with the second facilitator circulating across rooms.

Live proposal drafting

Participants draft actual sections of a concept note or proposal using structured templates shared on screen.

Peer review sessions

Structured peer critique using a donor-perspective review rubric — critique, refine and strengthen outputs.

Plenary synthesis

Group debrief after each exercise to surface common challenges, good practices and shared learning.

Reflection and action planning

Each day closes with a brief reflection moment and a commitment to one concrete next step.

Learning principle: Read the concept. See a real example. Draft your own version. Get peer feedback. Revise. Connect it to a live funding opportunity. Leave with something usable.

8. Contextualisation for Peacebuilding and Humanitarian Programming

A generic fundraising training is not what AFSC needs. The programme must be grounded in the realities of programming in conflict-affected contexts — where donors apply conflict-sensitivity lenses, where communities are the primary actors and where proposals must demonstrate locally led, evidence-based and trauma-informed approaches.

How the Training is Contextualised

AFSC thematic anchoring

All examples, case studies and templates are drawn from or adapted to Just Peace and Just Migration programming — not generic development projects.

Conflict-sensitive framing

Participants learn how to apply Do No Harm and conflict-sensitivity principles in proposal design, results logic and donor narrative.

Locally led language

The training emphasizes how to position local partner organizations as primary actors — addressing the growing donor preference for locally led programming.

Donor intelligence for the context

Specific sessions map out which donors fund peacebuilding, healing, reconciliation, youth empowerment and social cohesion in Somalia and South Sudan.

Real funding opportunities

Where possible, the training is anchored to live or recent calls for proposals relevant to AFSC's work, making exercises immediately practical.

Partner-level adaptation

Exercises are designed so that participants at both the AFSC staff level and the grassroots C/FBO level can meaningfully engage and produce relevant outputs.

Peacebuilding proposals must do more than describe activities — they must tell a credible story of change in communities shaped by trauma, displacement and conflict. This training ensures participants can tell that story compellingly and convincingly to any major donor.

Key Assumptions

•Participants have some familiarity with AFSC's programming areas and can ground exercises in real project contexts.

•AFSC will provide access to relevant programme documents, funding strategies or previous proposals to anchor the training.

•Local partner participants have basic written English skills sufficient to engage with proposal drafting exercises.

Key Risks and Mitigations

Variable participant experience levels

Training is designed with tiered exercises — a baseline concept note task on Day 1 helps facilitators calibrate depth and group participants for peer review.

Technology challenges in virtual delivery

Sessions will use platforms accessible in low-bandwidth environments; all materials shared in advance; facilitators available for technical support.

Limited carry-through after training

Action planning on Day 4 and post-training tool kits are specifically designed to support individual follow-through and team-level application.
